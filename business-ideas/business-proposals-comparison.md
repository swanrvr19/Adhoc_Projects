# Business Proposals Comparison and Ranking

**Summary:** Five opportunities were assessed for a healthcare actuary building alongside a full-time job; the best risk-adjusted starting point is ProofStack Health, a productized digital-health evidence-readiness service.

## Executive Summary

The five proposals are deliberately different business shapes. Two are high-value healthcare analytics services that could evolve into software, one is a productized evidence consultancy, one is professional education, and one applies forecasting to ultramarathon operations.

**Recommendation:** start with [ProofStack Health](digital-health-outcomes-evidence-studio.md). It uses claims analytics, financial modeling, program measurement, and technical implementation; has a specific buyer moment; can be validated without PHI; and is supported by unusually direct evidence that digital-health purchasers want outcomes proof and performance-based contracts. PHTI's 2025 survey of 309 U.S. purchasing decision-makers reported that nearly half were already using performance-based contracts, most planned to use them in the next year, 75% of contracts lasted two years or less, and more than half of purchasers reviewed offerings annually.[1]

The [Medicare Analytics Engineering Lab](medicare-analytics-engineering-academy.md) ranks second because it is the easiest low-cost, no-PHI launch and can produce revenue quickly, but free content and discretionary training budgets weaken demand. [MA Forecast Control Room](medicare-plan-forecasting-control-room.md) is the deepest experience fit and may have the best long-term specialist-company potential, but actuarial scope, employer conflict, plan procurement, and data security make it harder to validate as a side business. [NetSave Assurance](payment-integrity-savings-assurance.md) has excellent buyer economics but the worst launch friction. [AidStation Ops](ultramarathon-race-operations-forecasting.md) is inexpensive and personally engaging, yet its small budget pool and adjacent free tools make it the weakest primary business.

> **Important:** Scores are analytical judgments, not verified probabilities. Market facts are cited; prices, revenue, expense, time, and conversion estimates are assumptions. Before external outreach in the healthcare segments, written employer/IP/conflict clearance is a gate—not a later cleanup task.

## Scoring Method

Every category uses **10 as favorable**:

- Startup cost: 10 means lowest cost.
- Competitive difficulty: 10 means easier to differentiate and win.
- Regulatory or employer-conflict risk: 10 means lowest risk.
- All other categories: 10 means stronger/better/faster.

The directional composite uses these weights: fit 15%, demand 15%, ease 10%, startup cost 5%, time to revenue 10%, income 10%, scalability 10%, competitive position 5%, risk 10%, and overall probability 10%. “Overall probability of success” is an explicit judgment that partly overlaps other categories; it is retained because it was requested and captures trust/access factors that the mechanical categories miss. Differences below 0.25 points should not be treated as precise.

## Ranking

### 1. ProofStack Health — 7.50/10

Best balance of a real, current buyer requirement and a low-data entry product. The 2025 PHTI findings directly support the need for outcomes proof and performance contracts.[1] The proposed founder can sell a decision-ready evidence package before taking on PHI or building software. Main weakness: a solo vendor must overcome credibility and avoid implying independent validation while funded by the client.

### 2. Medicare Analytics Engineering Lab — 7.25/10

The easiest responsible side-business launch. Published prices show that professional and instructor-led technical training receives real budget: Databricks lists instructor-led offerings at $750–$1,500, while SOA lists 2026 PD Edge+ at $815.[2][3] The niche is defensible only if it changes a team workflow; a generic course will lose to free content and AI tutors.

### 3. MA Forecast Control Room — 7.10/10

The strongest subject-matter fit and potentially the best long-term specialist company. Medicare scale, annual CMS change, and established consulting products validate the need.[4][5][12] It ranks below the top two because the very facts that create expertise—Medicare actuarial work, models, clients, and methods—also create the greatest employer/IP risk. Written permission can move this ranking materially up or down.

### 4. NetSave Assurance — 6.40/10

The buyer problem is expensive and explicit. ZS discusses overlapping vendor inventories and net value; ClarisHealth sells orchestration around the same ecosystem.[6][7] However, a new solo firm needs access to sensitive plan data and vendor contracts, can trigger legal/provider/SIU issues, and faces long procurement. It is a strong later business after references or through an approved partner, not the easiest first side venture.

### 5. AidStation Ops — 6.35/10

The score is almost tied with NetSave but the business ranks fifth because commercial ceiling matters. It is safer and cheaper, and RunSignup's 2025 data indicates a large, stable endurance-event market.[8] Yet most races are small, OpenSplitTime already predicts live arrivals, and no source proves directors will pay separately for pre-race logistics forecasting.[11] It is worth a tightly capped passion experiment, not a default career bet.

## Best Proposal

### Recommendation: ProofStack Health

It is the best fit because it uses the broadest combination of the founder's durable advantages without requiring the most regulated form of the work on day one:

1. **Actuarial and financial:** net savings, credibility, risk corridors, sensitivity, and performance-guarantee economics.
2. **Claims and measurement:** eligibility, comparison groups, runout, selection, regression to the mean, utilization, and outcomes.
3. **Technical:** reproducible Python/SQL, synthetic test fixtures, Databricks/client-hosted implementation, and version control.
4. **Commercially timed:** RFP, pilot design, renewal, financing diligence, and at-risk contracts create a deadline and buyer.
5. **Side-business compatible:** the first paid diagnostic can use contracts, aggregate results, and metadata rather than PHI.
6. **Scalable core:** repeated metric definitions, tests, report generation, and contract patterns can become licensed intellectual property.

The recommendation is conditional. If the employer will not approve services for digital-health vendors selling to plans/employers, the fallback should be the Analytics Lab with a clean-room, synthetic curriculum. If the employer explicitly permits Medicare consulting and the founder has direct buyer access, MA Forecast Control Room may become the best economic opportunity.

## Smallest Step This Week

Write a one-page **Evidence Readiness Diagnostic** and send it to five Heads of Outcomes/CMOs—not a general survey. The page should show:

- one red/yellow/green audit of a fictional savings claim;
- the exact four-week outputs;
- exclusions: no certification, no favorable-result promise, no PHI in the diagnostic;
- a $1,500 founding diagnostic price, credited to a $7,500 founding sprint;
- a request for a 25-minute conversation about an active RFP, pilot, or renewal.

First, obtain written employer clearance. If clearance cannot be obtained this week, the smallest external step is postponed; use the week to define the conflict boundary and build the fictional sample.

## Three Customer Questions

1. **“Tell me about the last employer or health-plan deal where an evidence, ROI, or performance-guarantee question delayed, changed, or killed the deal. What exactly did the buyer reject, and what did that delay cost?”**
2. **“Which clinical or savings claim in your current materials would be hardest to reproduce from source data today? Walk me through the eligibility, comparison group, runout, program fees, and sign-off.”**
3. **“If a four-week sprint delivered a buyer-ready measurement charter, data specification, net-savings model, at-risk contract terms, and tested code skeleton, who would approve it, what budget would fund it, and what must be true for you to pay $7,500–$10,000?”**

These questions force evidence about a past event, a current artifact, and a real buying process. Avoid “Would you use this?” because polite interest is not demand.

## 90-Day Launch Roadmap

| Period | Actions | Spend assumption | Gate |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Days 1–15: permission and evidence | Obtain written employer/IP approval; form an exclusion list; conduct 5 buyer interviews; build synthetic virtual-MSK case, claim-audit scorecard, and one-page offer | $500–$2,000 | 4 of 5 buyers describe a concrete evidence obstacle; scope is legally/employer-permitted |
| Days 16–30: pre-sell | Conduct 7–10 more interviews; send 40 personalized trigger-based approaches; propose five $1,500 diagnostics and two $7,500 sprints; collect 50% upfront | $100–$500 | 3 proposals and either 2 diagnostics or 1 sprint sold |
| Days 31–60: deliver and standardize | Deliver paid work; measure cycle time and buyer objections; request an anonymized testimonial/case study; create v1 templates and automated tests; approach 5 referral partners | $500–$1,500 | Client rates output ≥8/10 and uses it in an RFP/pilot/contract decision; one referral partner agrees to introduce prospects |
| Days 61–90: repeat and narrow | Close and deliver a second sprint in the strongest clinical segment; introduce a 3-month $1,750/month retainer; document repeatable versus custom work; make a PHI/security roadmap but do not overbuild | $750–$2,000 | Cumulative $12,000+ contracted revenue, 2 paying logos, ≥40% gross margin after valuing contractor cost, and a repeatable module used twice |

### Day-90 decision

- **Continue and specialize:** two paying customers, at least $12,000 contracted, one repeatable use case, and a clear partner/channel.
- **Adjust:** buyers pay only for guarantee design, claim audits, or data specifications; reduce the offer to the paid component and test another 30 days.
- **Stop:** no paid customer after 15 qualified interviews, 50 tailored contacts, and seven concrete offers—or employer restrictions prevent serving the target segment.

### Range and downside

- **Downside:** $7,500–$15,000 revenue, $7,000–$10,000 expense, and 250–350 hours if only diagnostics sell. The economic result may be near zero after valuing time.
- **Central:** $50,750 revenue, $13,000 cash expense, and 545 hours as above.
- **Capacity ceiling without help:** about $80,000–$100,000 while maintaining a full-time job. Above that, project overlap, security, review, and client responsiveness become unsafe.

## Portfolio Logic and Decision Gates

Do not launch all five. Run one 30-day validation at a time:

1. Test ProofStack first.
2. If employer clearance blocks the buyer segment, test the Analytics Lab with synthetic data.
3. If interviews reveal stronger direct access to permitted regional MA clients, reassess the Control Room.
4. Treat NetSave as a partner-led later offer after security maturity and references.
5. Test AidStation Ops only as a capped weekend experiment or if two race directors volunteer usable data and a deposit.

The ranking should change when real evidence arrives. A paid deposit outweighs several points of desk-research scoring.

## Final Recommendation

Launch **ProofStack Health** first, contingent on written employer clearance. Its combination of an evidence-backed buyer need, a $1,500–$2,500 no-PHI entry product, $9,500 core sprint, and reusable measurement/code assets is the best balance of fit, launchability, income, and scalability. This week, create the one-page Evidence Readiness Diagnostic and ask five Heads of Outcomes the three questions above. At day 30 require a paid diagnostic or sprint; at day 90 require two paying customers and at least $12,000 contracted. A realistic first-year central case is **$50,750 revenue, $13,000 cash expenses, $37,750 pre-tax cash contribution, and about 545 founder hours**. If customers will not pay—or employer clearance fails—stop rather than building software, and test the synthetic-data Medicare Analytics Engineering Lab next.
